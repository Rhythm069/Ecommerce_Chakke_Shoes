Where It Started

A workshop above a hardware store in Porto.

Chakke_Shoes didn't start as a brand. It started as Mateus Aral, a third-generation shoemaker, deciding he'd rather cut ten pairs a week that lasted a decade than a thousand pairs a week that didn't.

He carved his first last from beechwood in a rented room above a hardware store, and the pattern number on that first boot — 001 — is still stitched into the lining of every pair we make, wherever the number on the box.

Today the workshop has grown to fourteen people and one very insistent rule: nothing leaves the bench until someone who isn't the maker has checked it by hand.

How a Pattern Is Born

From sketch to first sale.

The actual timeline a new pattern follows before it reaches the collection page.

01 / Month 1

Paper pattern

Every design starts flat — a paper pattern cut and re-cut until the proportions earn their place on a last.

02 / Month 2

Sample last

Three sample pairs are carved, worn by the team for a full month, and torn apart to check the stitching held.

03 / Month 3

Small batch

A first run of 40 pairs goes to the workshop mailing list before it's ever listed publicly.

04 / Month 4

Full release

Only patterns that get reordered by that first 40 make it into the standing collection.

What We Won't Compromise On

Three habits, held onto since pair No. 001.

Full-grain only

No corrected-grain, no bonded leather. If a hide has a natural mark, we pattern around it rather than sand it away.

Hand-stitched, always

Machines close some seams elsewhere in the industry. On our benches, a needle and waxed thread still do that job.

Built to be resoled

A Goodyear welt means the sole can be replaced two, three, four times without touching the upper. Most shoes can't say that.
